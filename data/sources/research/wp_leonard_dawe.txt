'''Leonard Sydney Dawe''' (3 November 1889 – 12 January 1963) was an English [[Amateur sports#Association football|amateur]] [[association football|footballer]] who played in the [[Southern Football League|Southern League]] for [[Southampton F.C.|Southampton]] between 1912 and 1913, and made one appearance for the [[England national amateur football team]] in 1912. He later became a [[schoolteacher]] and [[crossword]] compiler for ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' newspaper and in 1944 was interrogated on suspicion of [[espionage]] in the run-up to the [[D-Day]] landings.<ref name = "exfiles">{{cite journal |last1= Juson|first1=David|last2=Chalk |first2=Gary |title=Ex-Files: Leonard Sydney Dawe|journal=Saints Matchday Programme |page=48 |date=24 October 2009 }}</ref>

==Early career==
Dawe was born in [[Brentford]] in west [[London]] and was educated at [[Portsmouth Grammar School]], before going up to [[Emmanuel College, Cambridge|Emmanuel College]] at the [[University of Cambridge]]. In his final year at the university, he earned his football "[[Blue (university sport)#University of Cambridge|blue]]" when he played in the 1912 match against the [[Oxford University A.F.C.|University of Oxford]], scoring in his side's 3–1 victory.<ref name= "ABC">{{cite book | title=The Alphabet of the Saints|last= Holley |first= Duncan |author2=Chalk, Gary | publisher= ACL & Polar Publishing | year=1992| isbn=0-9514862-3-3|page=97}}</ref>

==Football career==
In March 1912, he signed on amateur terms for [[Southampton F.C.|Southampton]] of the [[Southern Football League|Southern League]], making his debut in a 1–0 victory over [[Plymouth Argyle F.C.|Plymouth Argyle]] on 30 March.<ref name = "Chalk51">{{cite book | title=Saints – A complete record|last= Chalk|first= Gary|author2=Holley, Duncan  | publisher= Breedon Books| year=1987| isbn= 0-907969-22-4| pages=50–51}}</ref> On his debut, he laid on the game's only goal for [[Percy Prince]]. The local daily paper, ''[[Southern Daily Echo|The Echo]]'', reported that "Dawe was decidedly plucky to 'get in it'." Dawe always took the field wearing spectacles and one of his lenses was broken during his debut game.<ref name = "exfiles"/>
 
Dawe continued to make occasional appearances for Southampton over the next twelve months, although his studies and teaching career prevented him from appearing more often.<ref name = "Chalk53">{{cite book | title=Saints – A complete record| pages=52–53}}</ref> In his eleven league appearances for the "Saints", he scored three goals, including two against [[Watford F.C.|Watford]] on 13 April 1912.<ref name = "Chalk51"/>

Dawe was a member of the [[Great Britain men's Olympic football team|Great Britain]] squad for the [[1912 Summer Olympics]] in [[Stockholm]], Sweden,<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.olympedia.org/athletes/700262 |title=Leonard Dawe |work=Olympedia |access-date=23 May 2021}}</ref> but was not selected to play.<ref>{{Cite web |url= https://www.fifa.com/tournaments/archive/tournament=512/edition=196996/teams/team=1889945.html|archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20120817000512/http://www.fifa.com/tournaments/archive/tournament=512/edition=196996/teams/team=1889945.html|url-status= dead|archive-date= 17 August 2012|title=Great Britain squad – 1912 Summer Olympics |publisher= [[FIFA]]|access-date=25 October 2009 }}</ref> He did, however, make one appearance for the [[England national amateur football team]] when he played against [[Ireland national football team (1882–1950)|Ireland]] in [[Belfast]] in October 1912.<ref name= "ABC"/>

By the end of the 1912–13 season, Dawe had severed his connection with Southampton and had joined [[Ilford F.C.|Ilford]] in [[North East (London sub region)|north-east London]].<ref name= "ABC"/>

==Teaching career==
In 1913, Dawe obtained a teaching position ([[Science education|teaching science]]) at [[Forest School (Walthamstow)|Forest School]] in the [[Walthamstow]] area of north-east London before joining [[St Paul's School (London)|St Paul's School]] based at [[Barnes, London|Barnes]]. In 1926, he joined [[Strand School]] in the [[Tulse Hill]] area of [[south London]], progressing to become the school's [[head teacher]].<ref name= "ABC"/> Dawe was described as a "disciplinarian and a man of extremely high principle".<ref>{{Cite web |url= http://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/England-History/Crossword.htm|title=The Crossword Panic of May 1944 |publisher= historic-uk.com|access-date=25 October 2009 }}</ref> At Strand School, he was known as "'moneybags", in allusion to his initials, [[Pound sign|L]].S.D. ([[Pound sterling|pound]]s, [[British shilling coin|shilling]]s and [[Penny (British pre-decimal coin)|pence]]).<ref name=Gilbert20>{{cite book |title= A Display of Lights (9): The Lives and Puzzles of the Telegraph's Six Greatest Cryptic Crossword Setters|last=Gilbert |first=Val |author2=Telegraph Group Limited    |year= 2008 |publisher=Macmillan |isbn= 978-0-230-71446-5|page=20 }}</ref>

==Military career==
During [[World War I]], Dawe was commissioned as a [[second lieutenant]] for service with the Forest School [[Officer Training Corps]] on 20 February 1915,<ref>{{London Gazette|issue=29077|page=1739|date=19 February 1915}}</ref> transferring to the [[Hampshire Regiment]] "local reserve" on 9 May 1916.<ref>{{London Gazette| issue=29583|page=4859| date=16 May 1916}}</ref> Whilst with the Hampshire Regiment, he served in the [[Mesopotamia campaign]] from September 1917.

After the war, he transferred as a [[lieutenant]] from a service battalion of the Hampshires to St Paul's School OTC on 29 April 1920,<ref>{{London Gazette|issue=31881|supp=y|page=4967|date=27 April 1920}}</ref> being promoted to [[Major (rank)|major]] with St Paul's OTC on 25 August 1926,<ref>{{London Gazette|issue=33205|page=6161|date=24 September 1926}}</ref> but resigned that commission on 16 October 1926.<ref>{{London Gazette|issue=33211|page=6617|date=15 October 1926}}</ref>

==Crossword compiler==
In 1925, he commenced compiling [[crossword]]s for ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' newspaper and was one of the first compilers to use "[[Cryptic crossword|cryptic]]" clues. The first ''Daily Telegraph'' crossword, compiled by Dawe, appeared on 30 July 1925<ref>{{cite book |title= A Display of Lights (9)|pages=13–14 }}</ref> – he continued to compile crosswords until his death in 1963.<ref name = "exfiles"/>

During the [[Second World War]] Strand School was evacuated to [[Effingham, Surrey]]. Dawe was living at [[Leatherhead]] in 1944.<ref>{{cite news |last= Gilbert |first= Val |date= 3 May 2004 |title= D-Day crosswords are still a few clues short of a solution |url= https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/uknews/1460892/D-Day-crosswords-are-still-a-few-clues-short-of-a-solution.html |work= Daily Telegraph |location= London |access-date= 15 February 2021 |archive-date= 28 November 2020 |archive-url= https://web.archive.org/web/20201128082041/https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/uknews/1460892/D-Day-crosswords-are-still-a-few-clues-short-of-a-solution.html |url-status= live }}</ref>

==The Dieppe and D-Day crosswords==
{{main|D-Day Daily Telegraph crossword security alarm}}
Just two days before the ill-fated [[Dieppe raid]] in August 1942, the clue "''French port (6)''" appeared in the ''Daily Telegraph'' crossword (compiled by Dawe), whose solution, ''[[Dieppe, Seine-Maritime|Dieppe]],'' was revealed the next day; the landing took place on 19 August. The raid ended in a major failure, with thousands of Allied casualties. The War Office presumed that the crossword had been used to pass intelligence to the enemy, but all the suspicions were eventually dismissed as a coincidence.<ref name="Gilbert20" />

In 1944, several codenames related to the [[D-Day]] plans, such as "[[Utah Beach|Utah]]" and "[[Mulberry harbour|Mulberry]]", appeared as solutions in Dawe's crosswords in ''The Daily Telegraph''. The inclusion of the codewords was initially suspected by the British Secret Services to be a form of espionage, but it was determined that Dawe had got the words from boys at the school, who had overheard them from soldiers.<ref name="Gilbert20" />

=="Quest"==
In 1992, Dawe's life was the basis for an album, ''Quest'', by the [[neo-prog]] band [[FC (band)|Final Conflict]]. The album is about an ordinary man like Dawe imagining he is on trial for the failings in his life.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.justforkicks.de/detail.asp?id=9002&uid=0&lid=1 |title=Final Conflict: Another Moment in Time |work=Just For Kicks Music |access-date=25 October 2009 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110719051443/http://www.justforkicks.de/detail.asp?id=9002&uid=0&lid=1 |archive-date=19 July 2011 }}</ref>
