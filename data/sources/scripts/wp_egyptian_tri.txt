{{Short description|Hieroglyphs representing three consonants}}
As part of the system of [[Egyptian hieroglyphs]], some hieroglyphs served as phonograms representing one, two, or three consonants, used purely for their consonantal values. This use as phonograms contrasts with use as logograms, where hieroglyphs represent an entire word depicted by the image of the hieroglyph itself that may also have the same one, two, or three consonants.{{sfn | Beylage | 2018 | pp=15-16}}{{sfn | Allen | 2014 | p=33}}

== Usage ==

Triliteral signs could be used by themselves to indicate the consonant sequence they represent, or they could more often be written along with phonetic complements, that is, they could appear with [[Egyptian_uniliteral_signs|uniliteral signs]] that represent part of their value.{{sfn | Allen | 2014 | p=33}}{{sfn | Beylage | 2018 | p=26}} Some examples of triliterals with phonetic complementation appear in the table below.
