The '''''Variations on the name "Abegg"''''' in [[F major]], Op. 1, is a piece ([[Variation (music)|theme with variations]]) for [[piano]] by [[Robert Schumann]], composed between 1829 and 1830, while as a student in [[Heidelberg]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Schumann in Heidelberg |url=https://www.schumann-portal.de/schumann_in_heidelberg.html |website=Schumann-Netzwerk}}</ref> The name is believed to refer to Schumann's fictitious friend, Meta Abegg, whose surname Schumann used through a [[musical cryptogram]] as the [[Motif (music)|motivic]] basis for the piece. The name Meta is considered to be an anagram of the word "[[theme (music)|tema]]" (''Latin''). Another suggestion is Pauline von Abegg. Apparently, when he was twenty years old, Schumann met her and dedicated this work to her, as witnessed in [[Clara Schumann]]'s edition of her husband's piano works.

The first five [[Musical note|note]]s of the theme are A, B{{music|b}} (B), E, G, and G. This use of pitch names as letters was also used by Schumann in other compositions, such as his ''[[Carnaval (Schumann)|Carnaval]]''.

It is composed of:

: Thema (Animato) ([[F major]])
: Variations:
:# (''energico,'' F major)
:# (''il Basso parlando,'' F major)
:# (''corrente,'' F major)
:# (''cantabile,'' [[A-flat major]])
:# (''Finale alla Fantasia. Vivace,'' F major)
