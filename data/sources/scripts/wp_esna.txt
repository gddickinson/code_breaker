The '''Temple of Esna''', also known as the '''Temple of Khnum at Esna''', is an ancient [[Egyptian temple]] in the city of [[Esna]], about 64 kilometres south of [[Luxor]]. The temple was mainly dedicated to the creator god [[Khnum]] and the goddess [[Neith]]. Other deities worshipped there included [[Heka (god)|Heka]], [[Menhit|Menhyt]] and Nebetu.<ref name="Hallof">{{cite encyclopedia

The surviving temple was constructed during the [[Ptolemaic Kingdom|Ptolemaic period]]. Building began under [[Ptolemy V Epiphanes]], while decoration continued under [[Ptolemy VI Philometor]], [[Ptolemy VIII Physcon|Ptolemy VIII]] and [[Cleopatra II]]. During the Roman period, a large columned hall was added, probably under Emperor [[Claudius]]. Its decoration continued until the reign of [[Decius]], in the third century AD.<ref name="Hallof" />

==History and architecture==

Only the large [[hypostyle hall]] of the temple remains standing. The original sanctuary and the other rooms of the temple have disappeared or lie beneath the modern city of Esna.<ref name="Hallof" />

The hall is approximately 37 metres long, 20 metres wide and 15 metres high. Its roof is supported by eighteen columns and six half-columns. The twenty-four columns and half-columns have decorated capitals based on different plant forms, and no two capitals are identical.<ref name="Hallof" />

The ceiling is decorated with astronomical images, including stars, planets, constellations and the [[zodiac]]. The walls and columns contain religious texts, calendars, hymns and scenes of rulers making offerings to Egyptian gods. Fourteen of the columns preserve parts of an extensive temple ritual.<ref name="Hallof" />

The temple contains some of the latest extensive [[Egyptian hieroglyphs|hieroglyphic inscriptions]] known from ancient Egypt. The priests of Esna used rare signs, unusual spellings and visual wordplay in the inscriptions.<ref name="Hallof" />

The temple contains the names and representations of numerous Roman emperors, including [[Claudius]], [[Vespasian]], [[Domitian]], [[Trajan]], [[Hadrian]], [[Commodus]], [[Septimius Severus]], [[Caracalla]], [[Geta (emperor)|Geta]], [[Philip the Arab]] and [[Decius]]. They were portrayed according to traditional Egyptian customs, wearing pharaonic crowns and presenting offerings to the gods.<ref name="Hallof" />

Philip the Arab was represented in two scenes. One showed him worshipping [[Montu|Montu-Ra]] and Tjenenet, while the other showed him presenting a ceremonial collar to [[Shu (Egyptian god)|Shu]] and [[Tefnut]]. After Philip was defeated by Decius, Philip's names were erased and replaced with the names of Decius. Some column cartouches of Decius were also placed over earlier cartouches of Philip.<ref name="Sauneron">{{cite journal
